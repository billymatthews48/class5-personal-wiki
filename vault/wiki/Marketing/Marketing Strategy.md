# Marketing Strategy

Billy's coursework in Marketing covered foundational concepts such as the 3 Cs (company, customer, competitors) and the 4 Ps framework (product, price, promotion, place). He applied these concepts to case studies involving Tata Motors Group, analyzing challenges like Covid-19 impacts and exploring strategies like segmentation, targeting, and pricing optimization.

## Key ideas
- Marketing fundamentally requires understanding the consumer to inform decisions on product design, pricing, and promotion. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS6 Marketing/Lectures/Week 1 - The role of marketing.docx|MS6 Marketing / Week 1 - The role of marketing]])
- The product offering has three levels: the core product, tangible benefits like branding, and augmented benefits like after-sale service. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS6 Marketing/Lectures/Week 4 - Product.docx|MS6 Marketing / Week 4 - Product]])
- Customer segmentation divides the market into distinct groups based on needs, allowing companies to focus limited resources. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS6 Marketing/Lectures/Week 3 - Segmentation and targeting.docx|MS6 Marketing / Week 3 - Segmentation and targeting]])
- Price is the only P that captures firm value, which is created by product strategy and communicated by promotion. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS6 Marketing/Lectures/Week 5 - Pricing.docx|MS6 Marketing / Week 5 - Pricing]])
- Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) helps firms assess customer worth to determine appropriate acquisition spending. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS6 Marketing/Lectures/Week 2 - CLV.docx|MS6 Marketing / Week 2 - CLV]])
- Tata Group utilizes both 'branded-house' and 'house of brands' approaches across its various verticals. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS6 Marketing/Exam/Final draft.docx|MS6 Marketing / Final draft]])
