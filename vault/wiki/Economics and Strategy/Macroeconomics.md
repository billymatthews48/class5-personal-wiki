# Macroeconomics

This collection of notes covers various aspects of Macroeconomics, ranging from the immediate global economic impacts of events like COVID-19 to long-term growth models and short-run economic fluctuations. The work explores concepts such as aggregate demand and supply, the role of capital accumulation, and the relationship between debt and economic growth.

## Key ideas
- COVID-19 caused the first global recession since 2009, affecting the global economy through transnational labor flows ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Macroeconomics/Assessment/Draft.docx|Macroeconomics / Draft]])
- Costly disasters like COVID-19 disrupt supply, demand, and productivity channels simultaneously across countries ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Macroeconomics/Assessment/Additional readings.docx|Macroeconomics / Additional readings]])
- The Solow-Swan model emphasizes that sustained growth requires capital accumulation and technical progress ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Macroeconomics/Lectures/Part II - Solow-swan model.docx|Macroeconomics / Part II - Solow-swan model]])
- In the short run, fiscal expansion can stimulate Aggregate Demand (AD) and increase GDP ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Macroeconomics/Assessment/Core readings.docx|Macroeconomics / Core readings]])
- The Golden Rule steady state maximizes consumption per capita by balancing savings and investment ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Macroeconomics/Supervision 2.docx|Macroeconomics / Supervision 2]])
- The relationship between debt-to-GDP ratio and economic growth is complex, with varying schools of thought ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Macroeconomics/Assessment/Core readings.docx|Macroeconomics / Core readings]])
- Inflation expectations can influence consumption timing and increase the demand for money, as per the Fisher equation (spelled 'Fischer' in the lecture notes) ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Macroeconomics/Lectures/Inflation and trade cycle.docx|Macroeconomics / Inflation and trade cycle]])
