# Negotiation

Billy's coursework for the Negotiations elective module involved reflecting on three negotiation exercises: Salt Harbour, MedLee, and Harborco. The coursework focused on analyzing his own behavior during these negotiations using concepts explored through further reading to generate new hypotheses.

## Key ideas
- Negotiations can be analyzed across three domains: content (what), context (why/when), and process (how/where). ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Negotiations/Session 1.docx|Negotiations / Session 1]])
- Effective negotiation requires focusing on underlying interests rather than bargaining over stated positions. ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Negotiations/Getting to yes notes.docx|Negotiations / Getting to yes notes]])
- Tactical empathy, such as labeling emotions, can build trust and increase influence during negotiations. ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Negotiations/Assesment/Readings.docx|Negotiations / Readings]])
- Negotiations should aim to create value, moving beyond being viewed as simple zero-sum games. ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Negotiations/Session 2.docx|Negotiations / Session 2]])
- In multi-party settings, it is crucial to identify all key stakeholders and understand their real interests. ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Negotiations/Session 3.docx|Negotiations / Session 3]])
- Active listening, demonstrated by techniques like mirroring, helps show empathy and encourages the other party to reveal more information. ([[raw/cambridge/Electives/Negotiations/Assesment/Readings.docx|Negotiations / Readings]])
