# Operations Management

Billy's coursework in Operations Management covered core concepts such as quality improvement, process design, and supply chain management. He explored frameworks like the Sandcone model and Lean operations to understand how firms create value and manage variation in both product and service delivery.

## Key ideas
- Quality is fundamentally determined by the customer, not solely by the firm's internal standards. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS5 Operations Management/Supervisions/Supervision 3.docx|MS5 Operations Management / Supervision 3]])
- Process mapping helps identify bottlenecks in processes, which is crucial for increasing efficiency. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS5 Operations Management/Lectures/Week 3 - Process design and digitalisation.docx|MS5 Operations Management / Week 3 - Process design and digitalisation]])
- The Sandcone model helps analyze trade-offs between competing priorities like Quality, Cost, Delivery, and Flexibility. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS5 Operations Management/Lectures/Week 2 - Zara and processes.docx|MS5 Operations Management / Week 2 - Zara and processes]])
- Lean operations aim for a perfectly synchronized process that operates at the lowest possible cost by reducing waste. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS5 Operations Management/Lectures/Week 6 - Lean Operations.docx|MS5 Operations Management / Week 6 - Lean Operations]])
- Supply chains are vital as they determine customer experience and are a major source of cost, risk, and innovation. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS5 Operations Management/Lectures/Week 8 - Supply Chain Management.docx|MS5 Operations Management / Week 8 - Supply Chain Management]])
- Statistical quality control, aided by control charts, allowed differentiation between statistical and assignable process variation. ([[raw/cambridge/First week/MS5 Operations Management/Process theory textbook notes.docx|MS5 Operations Management / Process theory textbook notes]])
