# Finance and Accounting

Billy's coursework in Finance and Accounting covered the fundamental processes of identifying, measuring, recording, and communicating an organization's economic activities to various stakeholders. He produced notes detailing the accounting cycle, the structure of key financial statements, and methods for project appraisal using concepts like Net Present Value (NPV).

## Key ideas
- Accounting involves recording, categorizing, summarizing, and communicating financial information to aid economic decision-making. ([[raw/cambridge/Exams/MS4 Finance and accounting/Accounting roadmap.docx|MS4 Finance and accounting / Accounting roadmap]])
- The process of accounting is defined as identifying, measuring, recording, and communicating economic activities to stakeholders. ([[raw/cambridge/Exams/MS4 Finance and accounting/Accounting textbook notes.docx|MS4 Finance and accounting / Accounting textbook notes]])
- Project appraisal tools include NPV, IRR, payback period, and economic value added, requiring cash flow forecasting. ([[raw/cambridge/Exams/MS4 Finance and accounting/Lectures/7-8 - cash flows and project evaluation.docx|MS4 Finance and accounting / 7-8 - cash flows and project evaluation]])
- Stock valuation can be calculated using the dividend discount model, which involves the present value of future dividends. ([[raw/cambridge/Exams/MS4 Finance and accounting/Lectures/6 - stock and bond valuation.docx|MS4 Finance and accounting / 6 - stock and bond valuation]])
- The cash flow statement is divided into financing, investing, and operating activities, tracking changes between balance sheets. ([[raw/cambridge/Exams/MS4 Finance and accounting/Lectures/5 - PVs and Cashflow.docx|MS4 Finance and accounting / 5 - PVs and Cashflow]])
- Retained earnings decrease when a company pays dividends to its shareholders. ([[raw/cambridge/Exams/1114l_M3_Assessment.docx|Cambridge / 1114l_M3_Assessment]])
